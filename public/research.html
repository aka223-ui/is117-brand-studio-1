01 / PROJECT RECORD

Research question

How can Ita-Tees express bold anime fandom while helping a gift buyer understand the design and judge whether it fits the recipient’s interests?

This review compares three relevant clothing sites before the next prototype revision. The existing Brand Studio storefront was already built on November 3; this November 10 research does not retroactively establish that it informed the original build.

Scope: one unofficial Evangelion-inspired white T-shirt classroom concept. No sales, checkout or customer validation. Existing Brand Studio decisions supply the audience and identity; new implications below are proposals unless marked observed.

Three clothing sites

S1 / HOT TOPIC

Neon Genesis Evangelion Eva-01 T-Shirt

Observed evidence: The title names the franchise and Eva-01. The expanded Details section describes front artwork with lightning, then lists material, care and sizing information. The page marks the item out of stock.

Interpretation: A franchise-led product page: playful insider language introduces the design, while a separate details section supports practical inspection. The artwork description is concrete, although a newcomer may still need context.

What it suggests: Name the reference and describe the graphic in ordinary language near the artwork. Keep the fan-oriented headline. Do not copy this seller’s materials, care, sizing or availability claims into a fictional product.

UT ARCHIVE Ghost In The Shell UT Graphic T-Shirt

Observed evidence: The product page separates the title, color and size selection from Description, Materials / Care, and Delivery / Exchange / Returns. Its description places the shirt in an SF Anime archive collection.

03 / PROJECT RECORD

Compare two positions

A / EXISTING DIRECTION RETAINED

Expressive fandom, with a welcoming explanation

Promise: Wear your fandom out loud.

Lead with visible artwork and a bold Creator identity; add brief context and a useful gift checklist. Hot Topic and Ripple Junction show ways of describing a named fandom graphic.

Tradeoff: insider references can exclude unfamiliar readers. Plain-language explanation must accompany the graphic.

B / ALTERNATIVE

Everyday graphic tee, led by practical comparison

Proposed promise: A graphic tee that fits your everyday style.

Lead with a quiet product presentation and grouped practical information, taking a cue from UNIQLO’s information structure.

Tradeoff: calmer presentation may understate the existing expressive identity. A real fit-led promise would also need garment measurements the concept does not have.

Decision: retain A because it matches the already recorded primary need—visible fandom expression—and the approved visual direction. Borrow B’s clarity of information. This is a reasoned design choice, not a finding that fans prefer A; no comparative audience test has been run.

04 / PROJECT RECORD

Two persona hypotheses

Assumptions, not interviews: these brief cards reuse the existing Brand Studio audience hypotheses. No ages, income estimates or customer quotations are inferred.

PRIMARY / ANIME FAN

Express an interest visibly

Situation: choosing a graphic tee for casual wear, a meetup or time at home.

Goal: show enthusiasm for a favorite anime through visible artwork.

Concern: whether the graphic communicates the intended fandom and matches their taste.

Page need: inspect the blueprint graphic and understand the concept’s limits.

SECONDARY / GIFT BUYER

Choose thoughtfully without knowing the lore

Situation: considering a gift for an Evangelion fan.

Goal: connect the recipient’s interests to a suitable design.

Concern: unfamiliar names and uncertainty about the recipient’s preferences or size.

Page need: a short artwork explanation and a checklist of things to confirm.

Chosen audience: anime fans remain primary; gift buyers provide a contrasting readability check. Issue #3 is evidence that one reviewer identified an explanation gap, not proof that every gift buyer has it.

05 / PROJECT RECORD

Look, character & honest persuasion

The existing live style guide sets a Creator character with a bold, playful, welcoming voice. The mechanical wordmark, lavender background, lime actions, tilted frame, hard shadows and heavy oblique headings continue that approved identity. Keep documentation body text calm and readable so another person can inspect the reasoning.

Reciprocity: the free gift checklist offers practical help without signup. Its intended benefit is helping an unfamiliar buyer confirm the show, artwork preference and size; no conversion effect has been measured.

Unity: the brand story frames visible fandom as a shared interest. It does not establish a customer community or justify invented membership numbers.

Truthful boundaries: preserve “unofficial student concept,” “not for sale,” fictional demo-price labeling and no affiliation or endorsement. Do not claim licensing, materials, fit, delivery, reviews or scarcity without evidence. Retailer claims describe their products, not Ita-Tees.

Trace evidence into a decision

F1 → D1 → C1 / ISSUE #3

Explain the graphic beside the product

Evidence: S1 names the franchise and describes its artwork; S3 identifies subjects and scene context. Reviewer issue #3 specifically says an unfamiliar gift buyer may not understand Unit-01.

Finding / interpretation: naming and explaining the subject is a useful pattern worth testing in this prototype. These pages do not prove an increase in comprehension.

Decision: implement a short explanation while retaining the expressive artwork and headline.

This student concept reinterprets Eva-01 (Unit-01) from Neon Genesis Evangelion as a simplified blueprint-style graphic. It may suit someone who enjoys the show and bold mecha artwork; confirm their preferences using the gift checklist.

Implementation: prepared in the storefront’s product description on the research branch; merge and deployment are pending. Review the documentation and code change.

C1 / planned reader check: ask an unfamiliar reader to explain what the graphic references, who might appreciate it, and whether the site sells a real product. Record their actual words or a faithful paraphrase. If they cannot answer, revise the explanation and retry.

Observed result: no unfamiliar-reader check or reviewer retry is recorded. Issue #3 stays open.

F2 → D2 → C2

Make product limits inspectable

Evidence: S2 separates practical product details into labeled sections. The existing Ita-Tees checklist says fit measurements are not supplied and the bag is a demonstration.

Decision: retain labeled demo sizes and limitations; defer a real fit guide until supported garment measurements exist.

C2 / planned check: ask the reviewer to locate the missing-measurement notice and explain whether an actual size or delivery promise can be determined. Success means they recognize the limit rather than infer a guarantee.

Observed result: the notices were visible during the AI’s initial page inspection; reader comprehension has not been checked.
